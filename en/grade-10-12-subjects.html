Grade 10–12 Subjects

Learners in Grades 10 to 12 take at least seven subjects: four compulsory subjects and three choice subjects.

In this section
  • Overview
  • Grade 8 & 9 Subjects
  • Grade 10–12 Subjects

Four compulsory subjects

  • Home Language — Afrikaans Home Language (Afrikaans classes) or English Home Language (English classes)
  • First Additional Language — English FAL or Afrikaans FAL
  • Life Orientation
  • Mathematics or Mathematical Literacy

Important combinations

  • Physical Sciences and Accounting require Mathematics.
  • Electrical Technology is taken with Mathematics, Physical Sciences and EGD.
  • Agricultural Technology and Hospitality Studies carry additional costs.
  • Mathematical Literacy limits some tertiary studies.

Choice subjects

Choose three subjects

This phase includes career-oriented education and training.

Subject guide

More about each subject

Afrikaans Home Language

Compulsory for all learners in the Afrikaans classes. The pass mark is 40% and the syllabus covers listening and speaking, reading and viewing, writing and presenting, and language structures and conventions. Learners who fail their home language fail the grade.

English Home Language

Compulsory for all learners in the English classes, with a pass mark of 40%. Reading at home is the best way to improve communication and marks, and the prescribed literature requires thorough study.

First Additional Languages

English FAL is compulsory for Afrikaans-speaking learners and Afrikaans FAL for learners in the English classes. The pass mark is 30%. Access to reading material in the second language at home makes a big difference.

Mathematics

A language of symbols and notation that develops logical and critical thinking, accuracy and problem-solving. It demands daily work and self-discipline, but closes almost no career doors.

Possible careers: Engineering, medicine, actuarial science, CA, IT, statistics, computer science

Mathematical Literacy

Practical and life-related: hire purchase, bonds, investments, map reading, timetables, areas and volumes and house plans. It is an alternative to Mathematics, but limits some tertiary studies.

Computer Applications Technology (CAT)

Using a computer effectively for work and study: Word, Excel, Access and PowerPoint, operating systems and basic web pages, plus theory on networks, the internet and information management.

Information Technology (IT)

The study of technology for capturing, processing and managing data, and problem-solving through logical thinking and programming. Mathematics is highly recommended.

Possible careers: Programmer, web developer, IT director, chief technology officer

Life Sciences

The scientific study of living organisms — from micro-organisms to plants, animals and humans — as well as environmental studies and diversity, change and continuity.

Possible careers: Nursing, physiotherapy, nature conservation, botany, zoology, microbiology

Physical Sciences

Physics and Chemistry. Mathematics is a requirement (not Mathematical Literacy) and learners need at least 50% for NS 1 and Mathematics in Grades 8 and 9, plus motivation and dedication.

Possible careers: Medicine, optometry, engineering, radiography, quantity surveying, occupational therapy

Accounting

The measurement, processing and communication of financial information. It calls for analytical thinking and daily work, and Mathematics must be taken with Accounting.

Possible careers: BCom Accounting (CA), engineering (costing), architecture, quantity surveying

Business Studies

Combines elements of accounting, finance, marketing, organisational studies and human resources. There are no formal prerequisites; with Mathematics and Accounting it is a solid platform for a BCom degree.

Engineering Graphics and Design (EGD)

A graphic language of lines, symbols and signs: freehand, instrument and CAD drawings, civil and mechanical drawings, descriptive geometry and the design process.

Possible careers: Architecture, engineering, graphic design, construction management

Electrical Technology

Dinamika focuses on electronics: from the principles of electricity in Grade 10 to semiconductors and integrated circuits. Must be taken with Mathematics, Physical Sciences and EGD.

Possible careers: Electrician, electronic engineer or technician, robotics, mechatronics

Life Orientation

Compulsory for all learners in Grades 10 to 12: development of the self, social and environmental responsibility, democracy and human rights, careers, study skills and physical education.

Geography

The study of the earth, the atmosphere and its people — human and physical geography — with practical map work every term.

Possible careers: City planning, geology, meteorology, environmental impact studies, hydrology

Agricultural Technology

The technology, tools, equipment and skills used in agriculture. The subject has a large practical component and additional start-up costs.

Hospitality Studies

Hygiene, food production, food and beverage service and customer service. The subject has a large practical component and additional start-up costs.

Tourism

Tourism sectors, map work and tour planning, attractions, culture and heritage, foreign exchange, customer care and marketing. The practical component counts towards the final mark.

Dramatic Arts

How human experience is presented to an audience in dramatic form: improvisation, vocal and physical communication, interpretation, and creating and presenting performances.

Visual Arts

Creative and technical development in the visual arts, combined with the study of art history and visual culture.
